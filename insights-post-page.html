1) Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (MTD ITSA): What Changes in 2026

Lead:

From April 2026, MTD for Income Tax becomes mandatory for many self‑employed individuals and landlords. The new system replaces traditional annual submissions with digital record‑keeping and quarterly updates.

Who Is Affected in 2026

The first phase applies to:

  • self‑employed individuals with income over £50,000
  • landlords with property income over £50,000

Those earning between £30,000–£50,000 will join in April 2027.

What Changes Under MTD ITSA

Businesses must:

  • keep digital records
  • use MTD‑compatible software
  • submit quarterly updates to HMRC
  • finalise their annual position through an End of Period Statement (EOPS)

Quarterly Updates

Instead of one annual tax return, individuals will submit four updates per year. These updates summarise income and expenses for each quarter.

Penalties

Late submissions and late payments will fall under the new points‑based penalty system. Repeated failures will trigger financial penalties.

How to Prepare

  • choose MTD‑compatible software
  • ensure records are kept digitally
  • review bookkeeping processes
  • seek support if unsure about compliance

2) Companies House Reform: 2025–2026 Implementation Timeline

Lead:

The Companies House reform continues through 2026, introducing stricter identity verification, enhanced data checks, and new filing obligations for directors and company owners.

Identity Verification

All directors, PSCs, and individuals filing on behalf of a company must complete identity verification. This requirement is being phased in throughout 2025–2026.

Stricter Filing Rules

Companies House now has expanded powers to query, reject, or remove inaccurate information. Filings must be complete, accurate, and consistent.

Confirmation Statement Changes

From 2025 onwards, companies must provide:

  • a registered email address
  • a statement confirming lawful business activity
  • updated PSC information where applicable

Increased Fees

Filing fees were raised in 2025 and remain in effect for 2026. This includes incorporation, confirmation statements, and changes to company details.

What Directors Must Do in 2026

  • complete ID verification
  • ensure PSC records are accurate
  • maintain up‑to‑date company registers
  • respond promptly to Companies House queries

3) VAT Thresholds and Digital Reporting in 2026

Lead:

VAT rules continue to evolve in 2026, with increased digital reporting expectations and closer HMRC monitoring of threshold breaches.

Current VAT Threshold

The VAT registration threshold remains at £90,000 (as increased in 2024). Businesses must register if their taxable turnover exceeds this amount in any rolling 12‑month period.

When You Must Register

Registration is required when:

  • turnover exceeds the threshold
  • you expect to exceed it in the next 30 days
  • you voluntarily choose to register

Digital Reporting Requirements

All VAT‑registered businesses must:

  • keep digital records
  • use MTD‑compatible software
  • maintain digital links between systems
  • submit VAT returns electronically

Common Mistakes

  • monitoring turnover only by tax year instead of rolling 12 months
  • mixing personal and business expenses
  • manual adjustments without digital links
  • late registration

What To Do If You Exceed the Limit

You must register within 30 days. Late registration may result in penalties and backdated VAT.

4) Payroll Year-End 2025/2026: Employer Checklist

Lead:

The payroll year-end requires employers to complete several statutory tasks, including RTI submissions, P60s, and benefits reporting.

Key Dates

  • 5 April 2026 – end of the tax year
  • 19 April 2026 – final FPS submission
  • 31 May 2026 – issue P60s to employees
  • 6 July 2026 – submit P11D and P11D(b) where applicable

What Employers Must Do

  • check employee details
  • process final payroll for the year
  • submit the final Full Payment Submission (FPS)
  • send P60s to all employees
  • review benefits and expenses
  • prepare P11D forms if required

Common Issues

  • incorrect NI categories
  • missing leaver dates
  • unreported benefits
  • late RTI submissions
